## Board Pitch

**Thesis (1 sentence):** MindBridge turns the class material learners already have into the format their brain can use, and every correction compounds into a learner profile and outcome record that a bundled platform feature cannot copy.

**Demo:** MindBridge Learning Studio, the working prototype: https://mindbridge-learning-paths.lovable.app/

**The case:**
1. Why now: Capable models made high-quality adaptation cheap, and platforms are bundling generic summarization into tools schools already own. Platform exposure scored 4 of 5 in the Class 1 diagnostic, which means the window to own the learner profile, the correction history, and the trust position is months, not years.
2. What's defensible: The flywheel scored 15 of 20, led by correction and preference compounding at 5 of 5 each. A competitor can copy adaptation features quickly; it cannot copy a learner's correction history, misconception map, and measured concept gains. The kill-switch audit keeps portability at Partial or better so a provider change never strands that asset.
3. The economics: At a $24 monthly learner price, AI COGS is $11.50 per active learner at 1,000 learners, a 52.1% gross margin that improves to 62.1% at 10,000 learners because about 75% of generation runs on the low-cost cascading path. Margin stays honest under stress: inference tripling makes the unit economics negative, which is exactly why the cascading discipline and the heavy-user school bands are in the design from day one.

**The risks:**
1. Trust / failure modes: Wrong content does not just fail, it teaches something false. Controlled by the Class 4 reliability contract: at least 92% of graded claims source-supported, hallucination under 2% of lessons, tiered confidence shown to the learner, human review on high-risk content, and a 150-row golden dataset gating every change.
2. Scale / governance: This is student data. FERPA and COPPA exposure is handled by course-scoped memory, consent-based profiles, and autonomy boundaries: MindBridge never grades, places, messages, or shares. The EU AI Act high-risk line holds only while those boundaries hold, and the governance policy says the deployment stops if a school asks for grading or placement use.
3. Competitive: Platform and LMS bundling puts about 50% of basic adaptation value at risk within 6 months. The defense is to be the evidence layer: own the profile and the outcomes, integrate with the platforms as channels, and never compete on generic summarization.

**The ask:** Approve Horizon 1 only: fund one quarter for a small product team to ship the persistent learner profile and run the 20-learner pilot, with a board readout at the pilot's end judged against the kill criteria written in Class 1. Scale funding is requested only if the pilot clears its bars. If it does not, the same criteria stop the work, and the eval set, the profile design, and the pilot evidence remain ours.

## M1 Baseline vs. Now
*Your 3-sentence AI strategy from Module 1 vs. what you'd say now:*

**M1 baseline:** Pick a painful problem and ship a capable AI product. If the adaptation is good enough, neurodivergent learners will use it, understand faster, and do better in class. The model doing the adaptation is the product.

**Now:** AI strategy is a portfolio of measured bets, not a feature list. MindBridge earns the right to scale only if corrections compound into a learner profile and outcome evidence no platform can copy, at a cost per learner that survives 3x inference prices and heavy users, under a reliability contract we re-prove on every change. Anything that cannot clear its kill criteria or its golden dataset does not ship, no matter how good the demo looks.
